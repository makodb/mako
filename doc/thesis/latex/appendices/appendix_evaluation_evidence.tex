This appendix summarizes how to read the evaluation evidence in the thesis. It
does not include source code, benchmark commands, raw logs, or raw measurement
files. Its purpose is to connect each major claim to the figure, table, or
metric that supports it.

\section{Metric Definitions}

\tabref{tab:appendix-metric-definitions} defines the metrics that appear in the
evaluation. These definitions are reader-facing; they describe how the thesis
uses each number rather than how the benchmark stores it internally.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption[Evaluation metric definitions]{Evaluation metric definitions.}
\label{tab:appendix-metric-definitions}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.30\linewidth}X@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Metric} & \textbf{Meaning in this thesis} \\
\midrule
Leader-side committed throughput &
The main throughput number: committed transactions per second reported by the
submission-side benchmark process. \\
\addlinespace
Follower replay progress &
The rate at which a follower applies committed transaction records to its local
state. It is used to check whether followers are keeping up with leader-side
work. \\
\addlinespace
Replay gap &
The difference between leader-submitted records and records replayed by the
slower follower at the end of the run. A large gap means follower work has
accumulated unreplayed. \\
\addlinespace
Worker CPU &
An activity indicator for Mako transaction workers. It helps show whether the
benchmark is keeping worker threads busy, but it is not a correctness metric. \\
\addlinespace
Simulated-disk bytes &
The modeled number of bytes charged to the controlled simulated-storage model. \\
\addlinespace
Modeled writes &
The modeled number of write operations charged to the simulated disk. \\
\addlinespace
Bytes per committed transaction &
The simulated-disk bytes normalized by useful committed work. It helps compare
storage pressure across backends. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}
