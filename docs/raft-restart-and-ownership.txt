================================================================================
 1. HEADLINE ANSWERS
================================================================================

 Q: How much of Raft is restart machinery?
 A: Small in the protocol, concentrated at the RPC boundary, and MOSTLY NOT
    LIVE IN PRODUCTION. Roughly 180 LOC of NotifyRestart machinery has no
    production caller at all.

 Q: Is `friend class RaftProxy` a problem?
 A: No -- because it does nothing. It is provably inert and can be deleted in
    the next commit at zero cost. [VERIFIED] The friend declarations that DO
    cost something are the two on RaftServer.

 Q: Do the messages cause ownership trouble?
 A: The message STRUCTS are plain PODs and are not themselves a hazard. The
    trouble is that there are two parallel sets of them from two different
    generators, one of which is stranded, and the shadow set is not even
    layout-compatible with the wire (bool vs bool_t). [VERIFIED]

 Q: Is restart machinery blocking the server.cc conversion?
 A: No. Separable. The four measured blockers for server.cc are out-params,
    the inherited recursive_mutex, Fiber::create_run, and the two-generator
    overlap -- none of which is restart machinery.

 3. THE RESTART INVENTORY, BY LAYER
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 3.1 THE NotifyRestart PROTOCOL -- registered in production, NEVER SENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  THE MECHANISM (per src/deptran/raft/NOTIFY_RESTART_MECHANISM.txt, verified
  against code): a node that restarts broadcasts NotifyRestart to every peer;
  each peer responds by tearing down and re-establishing its client connection
  to the restarted node, then invalidating speculative state.

    IDL          rcc_rpc.rpc:77-78   fiber NotifyRestart(siteid_t | bool_t)
                                     [VERIFIED -- not :75, which is a comment]
    wire types   rcc_rpc.h:716       RpcNotifyRestartRequest/Response
    dispatch     rcc_rpc.h:1138      __NotifyRestart__wrapper__
    proxy        rcc_rpc.h:1584      async_NotifyRestart + TypedFuture
    handler      service.cc:243      RaftServiceImpl::NotifyRestart
                                       -> commo->ReconnectToSite(...)
                                       -> svr->OnPeerRestart(...)  server.cc:7039
    sender       commo.cc:368        RaftCommo::SendNotifyRestart
    retry        commo.cc:430        RaftCommo::RetryPendingNotifyRestart
    gate         commo.cc:494        RaftCommo::HasPendingNotifyRestart
    state        commo.h:277-288     NotifyRestartState + notify_restart_state_

  (!) THE SEND PATH HAS NO PRODUCTION CALLER.  [VERIFIED]

      grep -rn SendNotifyRestart src/ tests/  yields exactly two call sites:
        testconf.cc:1141          -- RAFT_TEST-gated; option(RAFT_TEST ... OFF)
                                     at CMakeLists.txt:418
        rrr_transport.hpp:58      -- inside RrrTransportAdapter, whose factory
                                     make_rrr_transport is never CALLED
                                     (tests/raft_rrr_transport_compile_test.cc
                                      only takes its address)

      CONSEQUENCES:
        * The handler at service.cc:243 is registered on every production
          server and is never invoked. Registered-but-unreachable.
        * HasPendingNotifyRestart() is polled on EVERY election tick
          (server.cc:5130, inside the production StartElectionTimer fiber) and
          is permanently false, because only SendNotifyRestart seeds the map.
        * RetryPendingNotifyRestart (63 LOC) never executes in production.
        * Communicator::ReconnectToSite and RpcPeer::ReplaceClient are
          therefore ALSO production-dead: their only caller chain runs back
          through NotifyRestart. They are compiled in, never invoked.
          (This was a verifier correction -- the first pass wrongly called
           them production because it stopped one level short.)

      That is ~180 LOC across commo.h/commo.cc/service.cc/communicator.cc that
      a Rust port would be converting for nobody.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 3.2 WHAT PRODUCTION ACTUALLY USES FOR RESTART
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Two mechanisms, both real, both simpler than NotifyRestart:

  (a) THE ADMISSION GATE -- fail closed during the recovery window.
        rrr::Server::admission_ready defaults OPEN
            server.rs:877  ServerAdmissionReadyAtomic::new(true)
        Every Mako owner closes it immediately after construction:
            raft_worker.cc:337, raft_main_helper.cc:376
        and re-opens after startup completes:
            raft_worker.cc:473, raft_main_helper.cc:472/:475/:549
        Inbound RPCs arriving before that get SERVER_ERR_TRY_AGAIN at
        server.rs:1447 -- GATE 2 in docs/raft-rpc-flow.txt Part 4.2.

  (b) THE STARTUP BARRIER -- the production completion handshake.
        RaftServer::WaitForStartup            server.cc:2744-2748
        startup_mtx_ / startup_cv_ /
        startup_finished_ / startup_succeeded_  server.h:2221-2224
        signalled from RaftServer::Setup       server.cc:2735-2740
        (Both the first-pass map and my earlier reading missed this; a
         verifier flagged it as "the biggest miss".)

  (c) AVAILABLE BUT UNSUBSCRIBED: rrr already stamps a server_instance_id on
        EVERY reply header (server.rs:1277; the wire shape is
        [xid][err][server_instance_id][payload], server.rs:1274-1280) and the
        client stores and compares it (ClientConnection::server_instance_id_,
        compared around client.rs:1298). ClientConnection also carries
        on_server_restart_ : RefCell<OnServerRestartCallbackFn>.
        Raft does not subscribe. This is transport-level peer-restart
        detection that needs NO cooperating sender -- it would replace the
        entire NotifyRestart protocol with a callback registration.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 3.3 THE SERVER HOT-SWAP -- why service.h is 16% restart code
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Tests kill and restart a RaftServer UNDER A LIVE LISTENER. The service must
  therefore survive its own server being destroyed and replaced. That single
  requirement produces almost all of service.h's complexity:

    svr_ : AtomicPtr<RaftServer>              service.h:34
    ServerLease (RAII, copy AND move deleted)  service.h:75-108
        server_lease_state_ : AtomicU64        service.h:105
            packed: bit 63 = drain, low bits = borrower count
        server_replacement_active_ : AtomicBool service.h:108
    AcquireServerLease / ReplaceServerAndDrain  service.cc:342-431
        ~47 LOC of CAS protocol + ~42 LOC of busy-wait drain
    service_registry_ : static map<siteid_t, vector<RaftServiceImpl*>>
                                               service.h:29-30
    UpdateServer(site_id, new_svr) / GetPollThread(site_id)
        callers: testconf.cc only (GetPollThread's sole caller is
        testconf.cc:843)

  The registry is a vector rather than a pointer because single-group mode
  registers one extra RaftServiceImpl per stub listener against the SAME
  RaftServer (raft_main_helper.cc:366-391), and ~RaftServiceImpl consults it
  with >1 entries. So the multi-entry shape IS exercised in production, even
  though UpdateServer is not.

  There are THREE independent hand-rolled Weak<T> equivalents in the tree,
  ~250 LOC total, all solving "a callback may outlive the server":
    1. ServerLease                      service.h:75-108
    2. InstallSnapshotCallbackGate/Lease server.cc:333-420, held at server.h:2263
    3. async_callback_lifetime_          server.h:1964-1972
         a shared_ptr<{std::mutex, RaftServer*}>, nulled at server.cc:4473,
         consumed at server.cc:3715

  Notably, RaftCommo already solved the same problem CORRECTLY, and its
  comment says why:
      commo.h:285-286  "Futures may finish after a RaftCommo is destroyed
                        during RAFT_TEST Kill. Callbacks retain only this
                        shared state, never a raw communicator pointer."
      notify_restart_state_ : shared_ptr<NotifyRestartState>   commo.h:287
  That is Arc<Mutex<State>> in all but spelling. It is the shape the other
  three should converge on.


================================================================================
 4. THE friend DECLARATIONS -- ALL FIVE
================================================================================

  Excluding rcc_rpc.h marshalling boilerplate and vendored moodycamel, there
  are exactly five friend declarations in src/deptran:

  (1) commo.h:275   RaftCommo befriends RaftProxy       COST TO REMOVE: ZERO
      [VERIFIED] Three independent proofs it is inert:
        (a) grep -c RaftCommo src/deptran/rcc_rpc.h  ->  0
            The generated proxy cannot name the class that befriends it.
        (b) RaftCommo's ENTIRE private surface is two members --
            struct NotifyRestartState and notify_restart_state_
            (commo.h:277-288). Neither name appears in RaftProxy's body.
        (c) The dependency runs the other way: RaftCommo constructs stack
            RaftProxy objects at eleven sites in commo.cc, which needs no
            access at all.
      It also drops an accidental (harmless, lookup-invisible) declaration of
      janus::RaftProxy into namespace janus for TUs that include commo.h
      without rcc_rpc.h.
      DELETE IT.

  (2) communicator.h:42   RpcPeer befriends Communicator   COST: FREE IN RUST
      Unlocks ReplaceClient, Close, and direct locking of reconnect_mutex_ /
      closed_ (communicator.cc:161-162). In Rust this is simply module
      privacy: put both types in one module, mark the members pub(crate).
      Friendship IS module privacy. This is the well-formed use of the
      feature.

  (3) replicated_db.h:424  befriends RaftLabTest          COST: FREE
      Unlocks exactly one method (BootstrapEmptyStateMachine, used at
      test.cc:239) and zero fields -> pub(crate) fn.

  (4) server.h:1961   RaftServer befriends RaftTestConfig  COST: HIGH
  (5) server.h:1962   RaftServer befriends RaftLabTest     COST: HIGH
      [VERIFIED] Both sit immediately above RaftServer's `private:` at
      server.h:1963, with the comments "Allow test config to access private
      members for kill/restart" and "Allow test cases to access private
      members for verification".

      THESE are the expensive ones. test.cc and testconf.cc reach ~29-36
      distinct RaftServer members from another translation unit, including
      test-only mutators. Rust has no equivalent: the port must either grow a
      real #[cfg(test)] inspection surface or move the tests into the module.

      (A verifier correction worth recording: the first pass claimed ~34
       fields were private, but at least five of the named ones are PUBLIC --
       mtx_/site_id_/partition_id_ come from TxLogServer at scheduler.h:22-26,
       and min_active_slot_/raft_logs_ sit after RaftServer's `public:` at
       server.h:2777. So the friend-dependent surface is smaller than the raw
       field count suggests -- but still substantial.)

  SUMMARY: 3 of the 5 friends are free or zero-cost to remove. The Rust
  difficulty is concentrated entirely in the two test friendships on
  RaftServer, and those exist BECAUSE of kill/restart testing.

 5. THE MESSAGE SURFACE AND ITS DUPLICATION
================================================================================

  There are TWO parallel sets of Raft message types, from TWO different
  generators:

    A. rcc_rpc.h  -- generated by bin/rpcgen (Python,
                     src/rrr/pylib/simplerpcgen/lang_cpp.py) from
                     rcc_rpc.rpc. Structs live in class RaftService and are
                     aliased into RaftProxy. THIS IS THE WIRE FORMAT.

    B. messages.hpp -- 15 `pub struct` in Rust DSL blocks [VERIFIED count],
                     plus ~5 hand-written C++ structs for the ones the DSL
                     cannot express (AppendEntriesReq is C++ because it
                     carries a janus::Command payload; note it is absent from
                     the pub-struct list while AppendEntriesReply is present).

  WHICH SHOULD WIN: rcc_rpc.h, decisively, and messages.hpp should be DELETED
  rather than migrated -- with one exception (below).

  THE DECISIVE ARGUMENT IS THE ID LEDGER:
    rpcgen mints rpc codes with random.randint(0x10000000, 0x70000000)
    (rpcgen.py:334) and preserves them ONLY by re-parsing the committed header
    before overwriting it (load_existing_rpc_codes, rpcgen.py:291, called at
    :366). The committed src/deptran/rcc_rpc.h is therefore not merely
    output -- it is the ONLY durable record of NOTIFYRESTART=0x3df03452 and
    every other id. Any plan that retires it RE-RANDOMIZES THE WIRE PROTOCOL.

  TWO MORE NAILS:
    * messages.hpp is stranded. Its consumers -- transport.hpp,
      dispatcher.hpp, channel_transport.hpp, raft_node.hpp, rrr_transport.hpp,
      test_cluster.hpp -- are test-only or dead, plus tests/raft_messages_
      test.cc (518 LOC, a registered CTest at CMakeLists.txt:1859/1863; a
      verifier correctly flagged that the first pass called these structs
      "dead" when they are in fact TEST-EXERCISED). No PRODUCTION consumer
      exists. commo.h:7 includes it; no type in commo.h or commo.cc uses it.
    * The header's own justification is FALSE where it matters. messages.hpp
      claims the layout matches so the adapter is "a trivial memberwise copy",
      but bool_t is int8_t (constants.h:26) [VERIFIED] while the shadow
      structs declare C++ bool. commo.cc already hand-works-around this by
      deserializing into a bool_t and assigning across.

  THE ONE EXCEPTION WORTH SAVING: raft::AppendEntriesReply (messages.hpp:142).
  Already DSL, four u64 fields, NO bools -- so the memberwise copy into
  RpcAppendEntriesResponse genuinely is trivial. It is the natural return type
  for the OnAppendEntries out-param reshape (section 9, step 2).

 6. OWNERSHIP HAZARDS, RANKED BY PORT DIFFICULTY
================================================================================

 #1  TWO OWNERS OF ONE RaftServer -- see section 7, it is a latent bug.

 #2  AtomicPtr<RaftServer> + ServerLease            service.h:34, :75-108
     A non-owning raw pointer to an object owned elsewhere, made swappable by
     a packed-u64 admission epoch. Rust resists on two axes: the pointer (no
     owner, no lifetime) and the GUARD TYPE -- ServerLease deletes copy AND
     move (service.h:81-84), and that deletion is the entire safety argument.
     The DSL emits copyable aggregate structs for inherent impls and has no
     spelling for a deleted move ctor, so the guard cannot survive translation
     as written.
     RESHAPE: svr_ -> Mutex<Option<Arc<RaftServer>>> (or Weak + upgrade()).
     The preamble in all ten handlers becomes one clone() out of the mutex:
     the Arc clone IS the lease, the strong count IS the epoch counter,
     ReplaceServerAndDrain(nullptr) collapses to storing None, and the
     busy-wait drain disappears because the last handler's drop frees.
     Deletes roughly 130 LOC including server_replacement_active_.

 #3  THREE HAND-ROLLED Weak<T> IMPLEMENTATIONS, ~250 LOC   (section 3.3)
     RESHAPE: one rusty::sync::Weak<RaftServer>, captured by value into the
     callbacks; body becomes `if let Some(s) = w.upgrade()`. The shutdown
     assertion ActiveCallbacks()==0 (server.cc:4470) and the whole gate class
     delete. RaftCommo::notify_restart_state_ is already the right pattern.

 #4  TxLogServer::commo_ -- a PUBLIC raw Communicator* on the shared base
     (scheduler.h:24), narrowed by dynamic_cast on every use. Assigned from
     outside by whoever wired the process (raft_worker.cc:370,
     server_worker.cc:115, and on the restart path testconf.cc:851). No
     lifetime relationship to RaftFrame::commo_'s unique_ptr.
     RESHAPE: give RaftServer its own typed Arc<RaftCommo> set by constructor
     argument; delete the base field for the Raft path and the commo()
     accessor with its verify + dynamic_cast.

 #5  RaftTestConfig::Restart (testconf.cc:794-1149) is a HAND-MAINTAINED
     SECOND COPY of SetupInternal (server.cc:2447-2673), reaching 36 distinct
     svr_-> members through friend. Two Rust-hostile shapes at once:
     cross-module private mutation, and raw frame pointers captured into
     detached OneTimeJob/Fiber lambdas (testconf.cc:1100-1116) whose target is
     only release()d into the owning map eleven lines later (:1127).
     Every reshape of startup must be mirrored by hand or restart silently
     diverges.
     RESHAPE: extract the recovery half of SetupInternal into one named
     method -- RaftServer::RecoverAndPublish(RecoveryConfig) -> Result<> --
     and have Restart call it instead of re-poking heartbeat_setup_,
     startup_timestamp_, current_config_, rpc_ready_.

 #6  RpcPeer::WithClient hands const_cast<rrr::Client*>(client_.get()) to a
     closure (communicator.h:37-38); RaftProxy::__cl__ is a raw non-owning
     rrr::Client* (rcc_rpc.h:1260). An unenforced borrow across a lock.
     RESHAPE: with_client<R>(&self, f: impl FnOnce(&Client) -> R) -> R, and
     make the generated proxy carry a lifetime. The eleven construction sites
     are already correctly stack-scoped, so this is a CODEGEN edit in
     lang_cpp.py, not a source sweep.

 #7  malloc'd const char* smuggled through a tuple (raft_worker.cc:1063-1067)
     and freed by a different subsystem (src/mako/mako.hh:308, :313).
     (A verifier refuted the stronger "never freed" claim -- ownership IS
     transferred and the production consumer does free it. The hazard is the
     absence of a type-level contract, not a leak.)
     RESHAPE: make the queue element carry rusty::Vec<uint8_t>; both free()
     calls delete. Also delete the dead duplicate RaftWorker::un_replay_logs_
     (raft_worker.h:135, zero references) rather than porting it.

 7. A LATENT DOUBLE-FREE  [VERIFIED BY HAND]
================================================================================

  RaftFrame owns the server:
      frame.h:46    std::unique_ptr<RaftServer> svr_;
  CreateScheduler hands out a BORROWED raw pointer:
      frame.cc:208  return svr_.get();
      frame.cc:224  return svr_.get();
  RaftWorker stores it as a raw alias and DELETES it:
      raft_worker.h:121   TxLogServer* rep_sched_;
      raft_worker.cc:554  delete rep_sched_;

  If ~RaftFrame() ever ran, the unique_ptr would delete the same object a
  second time. It does not crash today ONLY because Frame::GetFrame returns
  `new RaftFrame()` (frame.cc:12-13) that nothing ever deletes --
  `grep 'delete rep_frame_'` is empty tree-wide. A leak is masking a
  double-free.

  IT IS RAFT-SPECIFIC. The Paxos sibling is correct:
      paxos/frame.cc:30-32   TxLogServer* MultiPaxosFrame::CreateScheduler() {
                               return new PaxosServer();   // UNOWNED
                             }
  so paxos_worker's identical `delete rep_sched_` is legitimate. The bug was
  introduced when RaftFrame was given unique_ptr members while the worker kept
  the Paxos-inherited delete.

  This is also ownership hazard #1: Rust cannot express it at all. You cannot
  drop through a borrowed alias of a Box.

  RESHAPE: RaftFrame::svr_ -> rusty::Arc<RaftServer>; Frame::CreateScheduler
  returns rusty::Arc<TxLogServer> in both implementors; rep_sched_ becomes
  Option<Arc<TxLogServer>>. The three deletes (raft_worker.cc:554,
  testconf.cc's `delete frame`, server_worker.cc's deliberate leak with its
  "skip manual deletion to avoid double-free" comment) all become drops.


================================================================================
 8. VERDICT: IS RESTART A BLOCKER FOR server.cc?
================================================================================

  NO. SEPARABLE. Treating it as a blocker would stall the port for the wrong
  reason.

  The four measured blockers for server.cc (docs/raft-dsl-status.txt s3) are:
      (a) out-params on OnAppendEntries          server.cc:5351
      (b) recursive_mutex mtx_ inherited from TxLogServer  scheduler.h:26
      (c) Fiber::create_run  server.cc:2680/2687/2699/2706/5115/5934
      (d) the two-generator wire-type overlap
  NONE of those is restart machinery. Meanwhile the restart code that IS
  inside server.cc is among the most DSL-friendly in the file: the
  parse-and-validate half of RecoverFromStorage is pure value logic, and
  recovery_manager.hpp is already substantially DSL and already LIVE.

  What restart machinery DOES block is WHOLE-CLASS conversion of RaftServer --
  and that is already blocked six other ways independently: ~91 default field
  initializers in the private section (the DSL has none, and C++20
  paren-aggregate-init compiles but MISFILLS), the inherited recursive mutex,
  the two friend declarations, and the AtomicPtr aliasing. Whole-class
  conversion is not reachable this cycle regardless.

  TWO PLACES WHERE RESTART GENUINELY TAXES THE PORT, both manageable:
    * A CHURN TAX: RaftTestConfig::Restart duplicates SetupInternal's
      ordering by hand (hazard #5). Pay it down first -- it is the
      highest-leverage restart-specific edit in the tree.
    * TWO DSL-INEXPRESSIBLE GUARDS: ServerLease (service.h:81-84) and
      InstallSnapshotCallbackLease (server.cc:396-420), both with deliberately
      deleted copy/move. They live in service.cc and an anonymous namespace,
      not in the bodies you convert first, so they are deferrable @unsafe
      leaves.


================================================================================
 9. RECOMMENDED ORDER OF WORK
================================================================================

  1. DELETE `friend class RaftProxy;` (commo.h:275). Zero cost, three proofs.

  2. FIX THE OWNERSHIP BUG (section 7) before anything else touches it.
     RaftFrame::svr_/commo_ -> Arc; CreateScheduler/CreateCommo return Arc;
     remove the three deletes. This is a real correctness fix, not just port
     preparation.

  3. DELETE THE NotifyRestart SEND PATH and subscribe to the transport-level
     detector instead. rrr already carries server_instance_id on every reply
     (server.rs:1277) and compares it client-side; wiring
     ClientConnection::on_server_restart_ gives real peer-restart detection
     with no cooperating sender. This retires ~180 LOC that has no production
     caller, including RetryPendingNotifyRestart, the per-tick
     HasPendingNotifyRestart poll at server.cc:5130, and the now-unreachable
     ReconnectToSite / ReplaceClient chain.

  4. RESHAPE OnAppendEntries from four out-params to returning
     raft::AppendEntriesReply (messages.hpp:142). Three wins: the DSL can
     express the signature, RaftServiceImpl::AppendEntries collapses, and the
     single most valuable stranded DSL struct becomes live.

  5. EXTRACT RaftServer::RecoverAndPublish from SetupInternal and have
     RaftTestConfig::Restart call it. Removes the hand-maintained duplicate
     and most of what `friend class RaftTestConfig` unlocks.

  6. REPLACE AtomicPtr+ServerLease with Mutex<Option<Arc<RaftServer>>>.
     ~130 LOC deleted, and the DSL-inexpressible guard problem evaporates
     rather than needing a kernel.

  7. CONVERGE the three hand-rolled Weak<T> schemes on one Arc/Weak.

  8. DELETE the rest of messages.hpp, the six abstraction headers, the *Cb
     commo variants, and the commo.h:7 include; shrink the DSL gate's block
     inventory in the same commit.

  9. LEAVE rcc_rpc.h generated-and-committed permanently. Convert only at its
     edge. If the codec must ever be Rust, change lang_cpp.py to emit DSL
     blocks -- never hand-convert its output, and never touch
     load_existing_rpc_codes.

  Steps 1-3 are pure deletion and a bug fix; they make the tree smaller and
  more correct before any conversion work begins. That is the right order.
